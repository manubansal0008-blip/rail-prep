Four-year review of Project Cheetah puts India's cheetah population at 52

A four-year status review of Project Cheetah released in September 2026 puts India's cheetah population at 52, as reported on 18 September 2026. The project completed four years on 17 September 2026; it began on 17 September 2022 when eight cheetahs brought from Namibia were released at Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh. Twelve more cheetahs were brought from South Africa in February 2023.

📌 Remember for the exam
  • The cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952; Project Cheetah was the world's first intercontinental translocation of a large wild carnivore.
  • Kuno National Park is in Sheopur district of Madhya Pradesh; Gandhi Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary in Madhya Pradesh is the second home for cheetahs.

First direct soil-carbon credit payments made to farmers in Punjab and Haryana

More than 2,500 farmers in Punjab and Haryana have received India's first direct payments for soil-carbon credits, as reported on 18 September 2026. Soil-carbon credits reward farmers for farming practices that store more carbon in the soil, such as reduced tillage and not burning crop residue.

📌 Remember for the exam
  • One carbon credit generally represents one tonne of carbon dioxide (or its equivalent) removed from or kept out of the atmosphere.
  • India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme was notified in 2023 under the Energy Conservation Act, 2001; Chandigarh is the shared capital of Punjab and Haryana.

India rejects Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission

India has rejected the Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission, saying it has no legal basis concerning territories that India claims, as reported on 18 September 2026. India has long held that the 1963 boundary agreement between China and Pakistan, under which Pakistan ceded the Shaksgam Valley to China, is illegal and invalid.

📌 Remember for the exam
  • Under the 1963 China–Pakistan Boundary Agreement, Pakistan handed over the Shaksgam Valley (about 5,180 sq km) of Pakistan-occupied territory to China; India has never recognised it.
  • The Shaksgam Valley lies north of the Siachen Glacier, near the Karakoram range.

  1. Aksai Chin
  2. Gilgit
  3. Shaksgam Valley
  4. Neelum Valley
Show answer

(C) Shaksgam Valley Pakistan ceded the Shaksgam Valley to China in 1963; India rejected the Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission in September 2026 as having no legal basis.
